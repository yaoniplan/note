- #### Use "english"
    - Capitalize the first letter of every proper noun (e.g. Bash).
    - Pronounce the preceding vowel with its long sound and keep the 'e' silent when a word ends in 'e'
        - `preceding` # Coming directly before something else in order or position. (e.g. in "**ate**", 'a' is the preceding vowel of 'e')
        - `vowel` # The letters *a*, *e*, *i*, *o*, *u* representing speech sounds made without obstructing airflow. (e.g. *a* in *cat*, *o* in *dog*)
        - `long vowel sound` # A vowel sound pronounced the same as its alphabet letter name. (e.g. *a* in *make*, *i* in *time*, *u* in *use*)
        - `silent e` (Or `magic e`) # An unpronounced 'e' at the end of a word that alters the sound of the vowel before it. (e.g. *cake*, *bike*, *home*)
    - Use 'does' when the subject is third-person singular
        - `does` # The third-person singular form of the verb *do* in the simple present tense. (e.g. "He **does** his work", "**Does** she know?")
        - `subject` # The noun or pronoun that performs the action or is described in a sentence. (e.g. in "**The cat** sleeps", "**The cat**" is the subject)
        - `third-person` # The grammatical perspective referring to someone or something other than the speaker (*I/We*) or listener (*you*). (e.g. he, she, it, they, or proper name like *David*)
        - `singular` # Refer to exactly one person, place, thing, or concept, as opposed to plural. (e.g. a dog, she, water vs. plural dogs, they)
